Imagine you are standing on a fast-moving bus. If the bus suddenly stops, what happens to you? You tend to keep moving forward, right? That's Newton's First Law in action!

Newton's First Law of Motion simply says:

> "An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion with the same speed and in the same direction, unless an outside force acts on it."

Let's break this down using simple language:

"Object at rest stays at rest": If something isn't moving (like a book sitting on a table), it will keep sitting there doing nothing until something pushes or pulls it (like you picking up the book).
"Object in motion stays in motion": If something is moving (like a cricket ball flying through the air), it will keep moving in a straight line at the same speed unless a force changes it (like the ball hitting the ground or being caught).
The Key Idea (The Unseen Force):
Everything tends to keep doing what it's already doing. We need an outside force (like a push, a pull, or friction) to make a change—to start the movement, stop the movement, or change the direction.

Relatable Indian Example: The Toffee Packet

Imagine you are walking along, and you are carrying a packet of toffees.

At Rest: When you are standing still, the packet of toffees is at rest. It will stay still unless you (the outside force) start moving or you drop it.
In Motion: As you walk, the packet is in motion. If you suddenly stop walking (applying a stopping force), the packet will tend to keep moving forward for a tiny moment before it settles down because of friction.
In short: Things are "stubborn." They like to keep doing what they are currently doing until a strong enough force convinces them to change!
